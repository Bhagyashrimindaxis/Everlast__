What is Paver Block?

Paver blocks are precast concrete units made to be laid side by side on a prepared base. Each block is cast, cured and finished in a factory, so size, strength and surface stay consistent. On site, they’re simply set in a pattern and the joints are filled.

Because they interlock, the load on one block is shared by its neighbours. That’s why a well-laid paver surface handles vehicles and foot traffic for years, and why a damaged section can be lifted and replaced without breaking up the whole area.

Our blocks are made from certified concrete, fly ash and sustainable aggregates. Thicknesses run from 55 mm up to 60, 80 and 100 mm, and the standard colours are red, yellow, grey and black.

The block is only half the story. A well-compacted base and a level bedding layer decide whether the surface stays flat, so we’re always happy to talk through the laying side with contractors before they order.

Applications of Paver Blocks

Pavers go almost anywhere outdoors. If you can park on it, walk on it or water it, there’s probably a paver for it. These are the places we see them most:

  • Residential driveways and porticos: Rubber mould blocks give a smooth, glossy finish in strong colours. They look sharp and cope with daily car traffic.
  • Plazas and building perimeters: The 12 x 12 inch block, in red, yellow, grey or black, suits commercial driveways and open plazas.
  • Public walkways: Short blast pavers have a rough texture that grips when wet. Busy footpaths are where that matters most.
  • Commercial parking lots: Square and interlocking blocks take constant vehicle movement. The pattern can even help mark bays and walkways.
  • Petrol pumps and bus terminals: High strength pavers, made from M40 to M60 grade mixes, are built for heavy loads. Fuel stations and logistics parks are the obvious customers.
  • Eco-friendly parking and pathways: Grass pavers have an open grid, so turf grows through it. Rain soaks back into the ground rather than running off.
  • Footpaths and metro stations: Tactile directional tiles guide visually impaired pedestrians. You’ll find them at crosswalks and platforms.
  • Pool decks, basements and ramps: Dollar tiles carry a raised circular texture for grip. They suit pool surrounds, commercial basements and anti-skid ramps.
