What Is RCC Precast Bollard?

An RCC precast bollard is a short, saturdy post made from reinforced cement concrete and cast in a factory instead of on site. It does one simple thing well: it stays put and takes the knock so that a wall, a pump or a pedestrian doesn’t have to. In practice, that means:

  • RCC construction: RCC stands for reinforced cement concrete, a material chosen for strength and long life outdoors.
  • Precast in a factory: The bollard is cast and cured before it reaches your site, so every piece is the same size and shape.
  • Made for traffic control: Its job is to guide, limit and protect traffic, whether that’s vehicles or pedestrians.
  • Fixed, not movable: Once set, the bollard stays where it is, which is the whole point when something needs protecting.
  • Standard size: At 750 mm tall and 6 inch in diameter, it’s easy to see without being bulky.
  • Cement fixing: It’s joined to the ground with cement, so it sits firmly in place.

How many you need depends on the site: the gap between bollards, the vehicles you want to stop and the space you want to keep open all play a part. We’re happy to talk through a layout with you before you order.

Applications of RCC Precast Bollards

Anywhere vehicles and people share space, a bollard helps keep them apart. These are the common places:

  • Footpaths and pavements: Bollards line the edge so vehicles don’t mount the walkway. They protect pedestrians without needing a full barrier.
  • Parking lots: They mark bays, protect walls and stop cars from drifting into pillars or each other.
  • Building entrances: A row of bollards at the door keeps vehicles away from glass fronts and shop corners. They also stop casual parking on the steps.
  • Residential complexes: Society gates, driveways and internal roads use them to control where cars can go. They help keep play areas and lawns clear of vehicles.
  • Petrol pumps and forecourts: They help guard pumps and islands from vehicles that come in too close.
  • Parks and public spaces: Bollards keep vehicles out of lawns, play areas and walking paths without spoiling the view.
  • Industrial yards and warehouses: They shield loading bays, gates and walls from trucks and forklifts. Repairs in these areas are costly, so a cheap post goes a long way.
  • Schools and hospital campuses: Bollards help separate entrances and footpaths from vehicle lanes. Safe drop-off zones matter most where children and patients are around.

In almost every one of these places, a bollard costs far less than the thing it protects.
